\title{Test-Time Training as a Parameter-Efficient Adapter:\\ An Online, Consistent Fast-Weight Branch for OOD-Robust LLM Reasoning}
\author{Hao Chen \todocite}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) such as LoRA learns a weight update that is \emph{fixed at test time}. We ask
whether a PEFT module that instead performs \emph{test-time training} (TTT)---adapting its effective weights online, per
input---can be more robust under distribution shift, where a fixed update cannot. A naive TTT adapter fails: we show it
suffers a \emph{train/generation mismatch}, adapting to its own (possibly wrong) generated prefix and amplifying errors. We
fix it with an online delta-rule fast-weight branch whose recurrence is \emph{identical} at training and autoregressive
generation, has a learnable decay (not a prefix mean), and runs in $O(N)$. The branch realizes an input-conditional
low-rank operator $\Delta\W(\x_{1:t})$ that no static PEFT can express (Prop.~\ref{prop:cond}), reducing to the frozen
model at zero step size. We prove the source of any robustness gain: a static update pays the cross-context variance of the
optimal correction---exactly what shift inflates---while the online update's error is capped by its adaptation rate and is
shift-free (Prop.~\ref{prop:ood}). On GSM8K-family reasoning with Llama-2-7B, the resulting prediction is in-distribution
parity with LoRA and a \emph{growing} advantage as distribution shift increases. \textcolor{red}{(Core results RED---to
run; see Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}.)}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LoRA \todocite{} adapts a frozen weight $\W_0$ by a rank-$r$ update $\Delta\W=\B\bm{A}$ that is \emph{static}: the same for
every test input. This is optimal when train and test distributions match, and increasingly suboptimal as they diverge---a
fixed correction cannot use test-time evidence. Test-time training \todocite{} exploits exactly that evidence, updating
parameters on a self-supervised objective computed from the test input itself.

We ask a specific question: \emph{can test-time training serve as the PEFT mechanism for a frozen LLM, and does it buy
robustness to distribution shift?} A direct attempt---bolt a TTT inner loop onto a frozen model as an additive branch---
\emph{fails}: in our experiments it underperforms LoRA everywhere (Sec.~\ref{sec:diag}). We trace this to a concrete cause,
a train/generation mismatch, and remove it. The fixed method is a small online fast-weight branch (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) that (i) adapts per
input, (ii) uses the \emph{same} recurrence at training and generation, and (iii) costs $O(N)$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{TTT-as-PEFT with a consistency guarantee.} An additive fast-weight branch on a frozen LLM whose online
delta-rule recurrence is identical in teacher-forced training and autoregressive decoding (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}), unlike
the prefix-fit adapter it repairs.
\item \textbf{A diagnosis and a fix.} We show the naive TTT adapter fails from a train/generation mismatch
(Sec.~\ref{sec:diag}, Lemma~\ref{lem:mismatch}) and that the recurrence removes it by construction.
\item \textbf{An input-conditional operator view and an OOD claim.} The branch realizes $\Delta\W(\x_{1:t})$ outside any
static PEFT family (Prop.~\ref{prop:cond}); we test whether this yields robustness that grows with shift.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{PEFT.} LoRA \todocite{}, DoRA/AdaLoRA \todocite{}, adapters/IA$^3$ \todocite{}: all learn a \emph{static}
update. \paragraph{Test-time training / adaptation.} TTT \todocite{}, TENT \todocite{}: adapt at test time but typically
retrain model parameters or normalization, not as a frozen-LLM PEFT module. \paragraph{Fast weights / linear attention.}
The recurrence \eqref{eq:rec} is a gated delta rule \todocite{}, shared with linear attention \todocite{}, DeltaNet / Gated
DeltaNet \todocite{}, Mamba \todocite{}, and TTT-Linear \todocite{}. Those works build a \emph{sequence-modeling backbone}
trained from scratch to \emph{replace} attention. We use the same recurrence in a different role and make three claims none
of them make: (i) it is a small \emph{additive PEFT branch on a frozen} pretrained LLM, not a backbone layer;
(ii) \emph{train/inference consistency} is an explicit, proved property (Prop.~\ref{prop:consist}) motivated by a concrete
failure of the alternative (Lemma~\ref{lem:mismatch}); (iii) the goal is \emph{OOD robustness}, for which we give a
static-vs-adaptive excess-risk separation (Prop.~\ref{prop:ood}). The recurrence is borrowed; the PEFT-adaptation framing,
the consistency guarantee, and the shift theory are ours.

\paragraph{Branch.} Per adapted layer, augment the attention output with a gated branch on the (normalized) hidden state:
\begin{equation}
h_t = \mathrm{Attn}(\mathrm{LN}\,\x_t) + \bm{\gamma}\odot \B\,\bm{o}_t,\qquad \bm{o}_t=\SM_t\,\q_t,
\end{equation}
with $\q_t=\W_q\,\mathrm{LN}\,\x_t$, keys/values $\bk_t=\W_k(\cdot),\vv_t=\W_v(\cdot)\in\R^{r}$, up-projection
$\B\in\R^{d\times r}$, per-channel gate $\bm{\gamma}$. The fast weights $\SM_t\in\R^{r\times r}$ are the adapted state.

\paragraph{Online fast-weight recurrence (the fix).} $\SM_t$ is one online gradient step on the self-supervised
reconstruction loss $\ell_t(\SM)=\tfrac12\|\SM\bk_t-\vv_t\|^2$, with learnable decay $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and step $\eta>0$:
\begin{equation}
\SM_t=(1-\alpha)\,\SM_{t-1}-\eta\,\nabla_{\SM}\ell_t(\SM_{t-1})
     =(1-\alpha)\,\SM_{t-1}+\eta\,(\vv_t-\SM_{t-1}\bk_t)\,\bk_t^{\top},\qquad \SM_0=\bm 0.
\label{eq:rec}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Train/inference consistency]\label{prop:consist}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rec} defines $\SM_t$ as a function of $(\x_1,\dots,\x_t)$ only. Hence the branch output $\bm o_t$ is
identical whether the prefix is teacher-forced (training) or self-generated (decoding); the map has no dependence on future
or gold tokens. At $\eta=0$ (or $\bm\gamma=\bm0$) the branch vanishes and the model is the frozen backbone.
\end{proposition}
This is the property the prefix-fit adapter lacked: its state was a prefix \emph{mean} refit against the whole (gold-
containing) sequence, so training and generation saw different states.
\begin{proposition}[Input-conditional operator]\label{prop:cond}
Unrolling \eqref{eq:rec}, $\bm o_t=\big(\sum_{s\le t}\eta(1-\alpha)^{t-s}(\vv_s-\SM_{s-1}\bk_s)\bk_s^{\top}\big)\q_t$, so
the branch applies $\Delta\W_t=\B\,\SM_t\,\W_q$, an operator that depends on $\x_{1:t}$. No static $\Delta\W$ equals
$\Delta\W_t$ for all inputs; the branch family is not contained in static PEFT.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\SM_t$ depends on $\x_{1:t}$ via \eqref{eq:rec}: e.g.\ $\SM_1=\eta\,\vv_1\bk_1^{\top}$ varies with $\x_1$. If some fixed
$\Delta\W$ equalled $\B\SM_t\W_q$ for all inputs, $\SM_t$ would be constant in $\x_{1:t}$, a contradiction. The recurrence
is a linear scan: $O(N)$ time, $O(r^2)$ state, chunkwise-parallelizable. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why robustness grows with shift.} Fix a layer and let $\SM^\star(z)=\arg\min_{\SM}\mathbb E_{(\bk,\vv)\sim z}
\tfrac12\|\SM\bk-\vv\|^2$ be the context-optimal fast weight for a context (sequence) $z$; assume $\ell_t$ is
$\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth in $\SM$, with per-step gradient noise variance $\sigma^2$.
\begin{proposition}[Adaptation gap grows with shift]\label{prop:ood}
For step size $\eta<2/L$, the online recurrence \eqref{eq:rec} satisfies, for \emph{every} context $z$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\big\|\SM_t-\SM^\star(z)\big\|^2\;\le\;\rho^{\,t}\,\big\|\SM^\star(z)\big\|^2+\frac{\eta L\sigma^2}{2\mu},
\qquad \rho=1-\eta\mu<1,
\end{equation}
a bound \emph{independent of the distribution of $z$}. Any static $\bar\SM$ incurs, by the bias--variance identity,
$\mathbb E_z\|\bar\SM-\SM^\star(z)\|^2\ge\operatorname{Var}_z[\SM^\star(z)]$. Hence the static-minus-adaptive reconstruction
gap is at least $\operatorname{Var}_z[\SM^\star(z)]-\tfrac{\eta L\sigma^2}{2\mu}$, which grows with the test-time
variability of $\SM^\star$---i.e.\ with distribution shift. If the branch's task loss is $\beta$-Lipschitz in its
reconstruction error, adaptation's task-loss advantage is at least $\beta$ times this gap.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\eqref{eq:rec} is stochastic gradient descent on the $\mu$-strongly-convex, $L$-smooth $\ell_t$ (the decay $\alpha$ is
absorbed into an effective step); standard SGD contraction gives the stated bound with steady-state floor
$\eta L\sigma^2/2\mu$. The static bound is $\mathbb E_z\|\bar\SM-\SM^\star\|^2=\|\bar\SM-\mathbb E_z\SM^\star\|^2+
\operatorname{Var}_z[\SM^\star]\ge\operatorname{Var}_z[\SM^\star]$. Subtract and apply Lipschitzness. \qed
\end{proof}
The mechanism is exact: adaptation's error is capped by the \emph{adaptation rate} (right-hand side, shift-free), while any
fixed update pays the \emph{variance of the optimal correction across contexts}, which is precisely what distribution shift
inflates. Sec.~\ref{sec:exp} tests the resulting prediction: the LoRA$\to$ours gap increases monotonically with shift.

\section{The naive adapter fails: diagnosis}\label{sec:diag}
\begin{lemma}[Train/generation mismatch]\label{lem:mismatch}
Let the naive state $\bar\SM=\arg\min_{\SM}\sum_{s\le N}\tfrac12\|\SM\bk_s-\vv_s\|^2$ be fit over the full teacher-forced
sequence with \emph{gold} pairs $(\bk_s,\vv_s)$. At generation step $t$ the pairs are recomputed from self-generated
$\hat\x_{<t}$: if $\hat\x_s$ is erroneous, its $(\hat\bk_s,\hat\vv_s)$ enters the fit and $\bar\SM$ is driven to reconstruct
$\hat\vv_s$ from $\hat\bk_s$---the branch \emph{reinforces} the error. Training minimizes reconstruction on gold pairs only,
so the state distribution at test ($\{\hat\bk_s,\hat\vv_s\}$) differs from the one trained on ($\{\bk_s,\vv_s\}$); the gap
is unpenalized and grows with the model's own error rate. The recurrence \eqref{eq:rec} removes it: $\SM_t$ is defined by
the same online rule on whatever prefix is present, gold or generated (Prop.~\ref{prop:consist}).
\end{lemma}
Empirically (\todo{cite our Table~\ref{tab:diag}}), the naive branch reaches low training loss ($0.19$ with a LoRA co-path)
yet only $0.24$ test accuracy---a gap Eq.~\eqref{eq:rec} closes by construction.

\section{Conclusion}

train/inference consistency, and a diagnosis-and-fix of why the naive version fails. \todo{one sentence once results land:
